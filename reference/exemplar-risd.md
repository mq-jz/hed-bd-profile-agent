About
Founded in 1877 in Providence, Rhode Island, the Rhode Island School of Design (RISD) is a private, nonprofit college and museum that enrolls roughly 2,500 students—about 2,000 undergraduates and 500 graduate students—from around 60 countries across more than 40 bachelor's and master's programs spanning fine arts, design, and architecture.
Established by Helen Adelia Rowe Metcalf to expand design education access for women, it sits on Providence's College Hill immediately adjacent to Brown University, with which it shares resources and offers cross-registration and dual-degree programs. It is highly selective, with an 8:1 student-faculty ratio and small studio classes, and is consistently ranked among the nation's top art and design schools.
From a funding standpoint, RISD's institutional profile is that of a specialized arts institution rather than a research university: its sponsored-research footprint is modest (reflected in its thin federal HERD numbers), concentrated largely in the NSF-supported Nature Lab and a handful of mission-oriented centers like the Center for Complexity, with the affiliated RISD Museum standing as one of the largest college art museums in the country.
Art & Object ranked RISD the #4 art school in the US in 2026, behind #3 MassArt, #2 CalArts, and #1 SCAD.

Strategic Plan
Strategic Planning
RISD is developing a new strategic plan to prepare for the institution’s next chapter, leading into its 150th anniversary and beyond. The process for creating the plan brings together students, faculty, staff, alumni, families, trustees, and external partners to help identify the priorities, opportunities, and challenges shaping RISD’s future.
This community input, gathered through surveys, workshops, interviews, conversations, data research, and institutional initiatives, will help shape RISD’s priorities and long-term direction over the next three to five years.
Mission Statement
We welcome, educate, and empower a diverse community of learners for lives of meaning, impact, and joy.
Values
We value the material practices of artists and designers as principal modes of engagement with the wider world.
We value the necessary and crucial contribution of deep disciplinary understanding to effective interdisciplinary practice.
We value collaborative interplay across design, fine arts and the liberal arts to cultivate deep literacies, to shape cumulative understanding, to transform thought and to expand making practices.
We value experimental, contextual and culturally diverse methods of creative practice and rigorous scholarship as essential ways of creating knowledge and engaging with complexity and uncertainty.
We value enlightened engagement with emerging and evolving technologies, along with critical reflection on the interests those technologies serve and the impact they have on diverse peoples, communities and the planet.
We value a classroom, studio and campus environment that advances principles of social equity and inclusion, environmental and climate justice, and equal access to resources and opportunities.
We value the development of lifelong skills that integrate the physical, emotional and mental well-being of our entire community.
We value the careful stewardship of our human, financial and physical resources.
We value our role as a place-based institution with a commitment to furthering progress in Providence and Rhode Island through mutually beneficial engagement with the community.

Federal Funding
Federal Funding Total 2017-2026: $2,483,239 (19 Awards) *Awards from 2017 also included, significant year with NSF
Link to Federal Funding History
Funding is concentrated in a handful of agencies whose missions align with RISD's dual identity as a degree-granting college and the operator of one of the nation's largest college art museums. RISD is funded as a design-research institution that engineering schools and manufacturers partner with, not as an art school asking for exhibition money. 
Two distinct funding streams are visible: research and capacity-building grants (largely NSF) and cultural, collections, and exhibition support (NEH and IMLS). Activity has accelerated sharply, with FY24 and FY25 accounting for nearly $1.5 million, about 60% of the total.

What the Funding Supports
Research capacity and innovation (NSF, about $1.66M). The largest awards fund institution-building: EPSCoR-style projects to strengthen research administration across Rhode Island's emerging research institutions, the Collaborative Research EPIIC SPARK partnership-building grant that Ossana’s gap analysis inquiry concerns, and EAGER biodesign K-12 STEM curriculum. Two awards reflect RISD's design edge, namely yarn-fabrication and CAD research and equitable nature-based climate solutions.
	•	EPIIC SPARK: This $400,000 NSF Enabling Partnerships to Increase Innovation Capacity (EPIIC) award supports RISD's participation in the four-institution Supporting Partnerships for Advancement, Research, and Knowledge (SPARK) collaborative, which helps geographically diverse primarily undergraduate institutions build research infrastructure, faculty incentives, and industry partnerships to expand use-inspired research and external funding capacity.
	•	Museum collections and preservation (NEH and IMLS, about $505K). Conservation and digitization of the RISD Museum's holdings, including Asian textiles, time-based media, historic wallpaper, and a collections-management system overhaul.
	•	Exhibitions and public programming (NEA, about $260K). Recurring small grants supporting individual exhibitions, artist workshops, and spatial-audio performance work.
	•	K-12 and educator pipeline (ED, $60K). A FY24 Department of Education grant supporting professional-development curriculum for elementary and secondary educators. This is RISD's only award outside the research and museum streams and its single point of contact with K-12 education policy.
AI-Recommended Future Opportunity Ideas:

RISD's edge is bringing design and material craft to a field dominated by engineering schools. Best play: position RISD as the design partner on a multi-institution proposal, with smart-textiles and wearables work that also carries a DoD soldier-systems angle. This builds directly on awards RISD has already won.
	•	Design for health and well-being (NSF plus the new NEA arts-and-health line). NEA's FY2027 Grants for Arts Projects now fund arts-and-health and creative-arts-therapy programs, with deadlines in February and July 2026. Paired with NSF's interest in human-centered and assistive design, RISD can run a two-agency strategy: design research on assistive devices, therapeutic environments, or rehabilitation tools (NSF), alongside community arts-and-health programming through the museum and Project Open Door (NEA). Diversifies beyond exhibition grants while staying true to the museum's public mission.

The NSF and Carnegie Corporation Support Groundbreaking Research at RISD
Dec 11, 2025
As universities across the US grapple with funding cuts for ongoing research projects, the National Science Foundation (NSF) and private backers are widening the scope of their investments to include Emerging Research Institutions (ERIs) like RISD. ERIs are defined as institutions of higher education with research programs that operate with limited federal funding.  
In recent months, RISD faculty and staff members have procured grants totaling over $1.6 million supporting the study of everything from inclusive research approaches to carbon exchange in the Antarctic’s Southern Ocean to automated 3D textile manufacturing. “RISD is leading the charge among art and design schools that recognize the unique contributions artists and designers can make to all kinds of research,” says Director of Academic and Creative Partnerships Katherine Cooper.
