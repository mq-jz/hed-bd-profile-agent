===== SECTION: Lobbying Disclosures =====
The University of Pennsylvania lobbies the federal government primarily through its in-house Office of Government and Community Affairs. No outside lobbying firm was identified in the disclosures reviewed here [verify: run a client search for "Trustees of the University of Pennsylvania" and "University of Pennsylvania Health System" at lda.senate.gov to confirm whether any registered outside firms are retained].

Reported federal lobbying spending was $640,000 in 2024, up roughly 37.5 percent from 2023, and included $180,000 in the fourth quarter of 2024, Penn's highest single-quarter total since 2019 (source: OpenSecrets org profile D000022295 and The Daily Pennsylvanian, February 2025; the OpenSecrets page itself returned a 403 on fetch, so the full year-by-year series is unconfirmed [verify: pull the annual totals directly from lda.senate.gov filings]). For context, Penn ranked second among Ivy League institutions in 2024 lobbying spend, behind Cornell at $660,000.

Named in-house lobbyists include William Andersen, Associate Vice President of Federal Affairs, and Kristen Molloy, University of Pennsylvania Health System Corporate Director for Government and Community Relations [verify: current LDA registrant list].

Reported issue areas: education and higher education, medical and disease research and clinical labs, Medicare and Medicaid, general health issues, and veterans. Specific matters cited include graduate medical education and federal drug-pricing legislation.

BD read: Penn's federal engagement is oriented toward research funding and health policy, not project earmarks. A sophisticated in-house shop already covers Washington, so M&Q's opening is in specialized grant strategy and funding diversification rather than basic government relations.

===== SECTION: Congressionally Directed Funding =====
Sen. Dave McCormick (R-PA) and Sen. John Fetterman (D-PA) represent the University of Pennsylvania. Both participate in the congressionally directed spending (earmarks) process and publish annual CDS request pages and appropriations guidance. Neither sits on the Senate Appropriations Committee, which limits their direct leverage over the earmark tables: McCormick serves on Banking, Housing, and Urban Affairs; Energy and Natural Resources; Foreign Relations; the Special Committee on Aging; and the Joint Economic Committee. Fetterman serves on Agriculture, Nutrition, and Forestry; Commerce, Science, and Transportation; Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs; and the Helsinki Commission (source: senate.gov committee assignments, 119th Congress).

Neither Senator has secured a publicly disclosed earmark on behalf of the University of Pennsylvania in the cycles reviewed. The University of Pennsylvania does not appear in Sen. Fetterman's FY24 submitted appropriations projects or in Sen. McCormick's FY2026 CDS request list; both lists include other Pennsylvania institutions (Penn State, Temple University Hospital, Thomas Jefferson University, the Wistar Institute, and PASSHE schools), but not Penn itself. This is consistent with an elite R1 that wins peer-reviewed competitive awards rather than pursuing earmarks [verify: check the remaining cycles (Fetterman FY25, FY26, FY27; McCormick FY2027) and the House Community Project Funding disclosure tables for "University of Pennsylvania" and "Penn Medicine," and take care not to confuse Penn with Penn State or the PASSHE "University of Pennsylvania" system schools].
