===== SECTION: Strategic Plan =====
Penn's operative strategic framework is In Principle and Practice: Penn's Focus on Tomorrow, released in November 2023 under then-President Liz Magill after a yearlong planning process; it succeeded the Penn Compact that guided the Gutmann administration. The framework is built on four principles (the Anchored University, the Interwoven University, the Inventive University, the Engaged University) and five practices (accelerate interdisciplinary pursuits, lead on great challenges of our time, grow opportunity and strengthen affinity, deepen connection with neighbors and the world, foster leadership and service). It names climate as an existential challenge demanding an all-in University effort, aims to cement Penn and the Philadelphia region as an epicenter for life sciences and health research, and commits Penn to lead on data and AI, democracy, and West Philadelphia community engagement. The framework survived the December 2023 presidential transition and continues to guide the FY2026 University operating budget. Link: https://in-principle-and-practice.upenn.edu/

Implementation now runs through Penn Forward, a University-wide initiative President J. Larry Jameson launched on September 8, 2025 to operationalize the framework rather than replace it. Six working groups (Undergraduate Education and Innovation; Graduate and Professional Training; Research Strategy and Financing; Global Opportunity and New Markets; Access, Affordability, and Value; Operational Transformation) produced more than 30 recommendations; on April 15, 2026 Jameson announced nine priority initiatives: Penn Experience, Penn Quaker Commitment, Penn Health Advantage, Penn Works Better, Penn Partners for Impact, Penn AI, Penn Learning for Life, Penn San Francisco, and Penn Global. The effort is organized by Jameson, Provost John L. Jackson Jr., and Executive Vice President Mark Dingfield, with coordination led by David A. Asch (Senior Vice President for Strategic Initiatives) and Thomas Murphy (Senior Vice President and Chief Transformation Officer). Link: https://www.upenn.edu/pennforward

Through the M&Q lens: a standing Research Strategy and Financing working group is an explicit acknowledgment that the sponsored-research financing model is under stress. Penn's federal receipts fell roughly 7 percent to $948M in FY2025 (Daily Pennsylvanian analysis, April 2026) amid NIH indirect-cost litigation and grant terminations, and the Penn Forward initiatives read as revenue diversification: industry and strategic partnerships (Penn Partners for Impact), AI (Penn AI), lifelong learning revenue (Penn Learning for Life), and geographic expansion (Penn San Francisco, Penn Global). Penn is an R1 with mature central government-relations and sponsored-programs operations, so the M&Q angle is not capacity building as with the practice's usual non-R1/R2 targets; the realistic openings are targeted federal-strategy work in a volatile funding environment (agency diversification, congressional strategy, defense and non-NIH streams) or unit-level support for individual schools and centers standing up new initiatives [inferred].

===== SECTION: Mission Statement =====
"The University of Pennsylvania is a scholarly community that advances discovery and opportunity toward a better future for all."

Penn announced this mission statement, together with its university values, to the campus community on September 10, 2024. Penn does not publish a separate vision statement; the closest analogue is the aspiration language of the In Principle and Practice framework.

===== SECTION: Values =====
Penn's stated university values (announced September 10, 2024):

- Excellence
- Freedom of inquiry and expression
- Respect

The values statement adds that Penn's culture is inspired by founder Benjamin Franklin: open-minded and curious, inventive and practical, exhibiting brilliance across fields, imperfect but self-improving, and relentlessly focused on enhancing social good.

===== SECTION: Centers and Institutes =====
Penn operates well over one hundred centers and institutes across its twelve schools; the list below is deliberately selective, limited to flagship and federally funded centers most relevant to a funding conversation.

- Penn Institute for RNA Innovation: founded 2021, directed by Nobel laureate Drew Weissman; home base for the mRNA vaccine research enterprise, spanning basic to translational RNA science at One uCity Square
- Abramson Cancer Center: NCI-designated Comprehensive Cancer Center, a designation held continuously since 1973; one of the nation's largest cancer research and care enterprises
- Leonard Davis Institute of Health Economics: founded 1967; Penn's hub for health policy, health economics, and health services research across schools
- Singh Center for Nanotechnology: joint School of Engineering and School of Arts and Sciences facility; NSF-funded Mid-Atlantic Nanotechnology Hub (a $5M NSF award) within the National Nanotechnology Coordinated Infrastructure network, open to academic and industrial users
- Laboratory for Research on the Structure of Matter (LRSM): Penn's materials research center, operating since 1960 and host of an NSF Materials Research Science and Engineering Center (MRSEC) since 1996, with five interdisciplinary research groups
- Kleinman Center for Energy Policy: established 2014 in the Stuart Weitzman School of Design; energy policy research and translation
- Vagelos Institute for Energy Science and Technology: launched 2016 and permanently endowed by the Vagelos family; energy science and technology research
- Annenberg Public Policy Center: policy-relevant communication research; houses the Penn Center for Science, Sustainability, and the Media
- Perry World House: campus-wide global affairs and policy engagement hub drawing on all twelve schools
- Penn Institute for Urban Research: cross-disciplinary research on cities and urban policy
- Netter Center for Community Partnerships: founded 1992; Penn's primary anchor-institution vehicle for West Philadelphia community engagement, directly tied to the Anchored University and Engaged University principles of the strategic framework

===== SECTION: Academic Programs =====
Penn enrolls undergraduates in four schools and offers graduate and professional degrees across twelve; at this scale the lists below are abbreviated to the structure and the programs most relevant to a funding conversation.

Undergraduate (more than 90 majors across four schools):
- School of Arts and Sciences (the College): 28 departments spanning humanities, social sciences, and natural sciences
- School of Engineering and Applied Science: founded 1852; engineering and applied science majors including bioengineering and computer science
- School of Nursing: BSN; consistently among the top-ranked nursing schools nationally
- Wharton School: BS in Economics
- Signature coordinated dual-degree programs include the Vagelos Program in Life Sciences and Management, the Vagelos Integrated Program in Energy Research (VIPER), and the Vagelos Scholars Program in the Molecular Life Sciences [verify: full roster of coordinated dual-degree programs at admissions.upenn.edu]

Graduate and professional (twelve schools):
- Perelman School of Medicine (America's first medical school), Penn Carey Law, Wharton (MBA and doctoral programs), School of Dental Medicine (est. 1878), School of Veterinary Medicine, School of Nursing, Graduate School of Education, School of Engineering and Applied Science, School of Arts and Sciences, Annenberg School for Communication, Stuart Weitzman School of Design, School of Social Policy and Practice
- Several professional schools (including Medicine, Nursing, Communication, Dental, Veterinary, and Law) rank in the national top five in their fields
- Newest degree: Wharton MS in Quantitative Finance, its first new degree in 50 years, launching with a first cohort in fall 2026 (funded by a $60M gift, see Recent News)

===== SECTION: Recent News =====
- Federal government restores $175M to Penn after Title IX resolution (July 1, 2025). The Trump administration released about $175M in federal funding frozen in March 2025 over transgender-athlete policy, after Penn agreed to a Title IX resolution with the Department of Education that included adopting biology-based definitions for male and female in athletics and removing Lia Thomas's individual swimming records. Penn chose settlement over litigation, a notable data point on its federal posture.
- President Jameson launches Penn Forward (September 8, 2025). Six months after trustees made him Penn's tenth president (March 2025 vote, term through June 2027), Jameson launched a university-wide strategic initiative with six working groups, including one dedicated to Research Strategy and Financing, to operationalize the In Principle and Practice framework.
- Wharton receives largest gift in its history: $60M from Bruce I. Jacobs (September 15, 2025). The gift from the Jacobs Levy Equity Management co-founder launches a Master of Science in Quantitative Finance, Wharton's first new degree in 50 years, with the first cohort starting fall 2026. Jacobs's lifetime giving to Penn and Wharton exceeds $80M.
- Penn rejects the Trump administration's higher education compact (October 16, 2025). Penn became the third of nine invited universities to decline the administration's proposed compact offering preferential federal funding consideration in exchange for commitments on tuition, admissions, and enrollment; Jameson's letter cited areas of alignment alongside substantive concerns. Together with the July Title IX settlement, this frames Penn's calibrated posture toward the federal government.
- Penn Forward advances nine priority initiatives (April 15, 2026). From more than 30 working-group recommendations, Jameson announced nine initiatives for immediate action, including Penn AI, Penn Partners for Impact (discovery through strategic partnerships), Penn Learning for Life, Penn San Francisco (a planned West Coast hub), and Penn Global; each is a potential locus of new sponsored-program and partnership activity.
- Penn's federal funding fell about 7 percent in FY2025 (Daily Pennsylvanian analysis, April 2026). Penn received $948M in federal funds in FY2025, down nearly 7 percent year over year amid NIH grant terminations and indirect-cost uncertainty; Penn has responded with hiring freezes, roughly 4 percent budget cuts, and sharply smaller PhD cohorts (Perelman down 35 percent, Arts and Sciences down 33 percent for fall 2025). Penn has warned an NIH 15 percent indirect-cost cap would cost it roughly $240M annually; the cap was held unenforceable on appeal in January 2026 while litigation proceeds.
