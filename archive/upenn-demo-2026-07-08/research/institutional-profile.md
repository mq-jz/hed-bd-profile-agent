===== SECTION: About =====
Founded in 1740 by Benjamin Franklin, the University of Pennsylvania (Penn) is a private, nonprofit Ivy League research university in Philadelphia, PA, enrolling 29,384 total students across 12 schools as of Fall 2025 (10,325 full-time undergraduates and 14,006 full-time graduate/professional students, per Penn's institutional facts page). Penn is a charter member of the Ivy League, a founding member of the Association of American Universities (1900), and a Research 1 institution under the 2025 Carnegie Classification. Its funding profile is the inverse of the HED practice's usual non-R1/R2 client: Penn operates one of the largest sponsored-research enterprises in the country, with a university-reported research budget of $1.33 billion (FY2026) and $1.34 billion in sponsored-project awards (FY2024-25), administered by a mature in-house research infrastructure. The BD case is therefore not grants capacity building but targeted plays: unit-level or school-level support, government relations and appropriations strategy, or foundation work in pockets the central apparatus underserves.

Penn received over $1 billion in federal funding in FY2024 across 2,143 grants and cooperative agreements from 16 departments and agencies, the majority from HHS/NIH, followed by NSF, DOD, and the Department of Education (Daily Pennsylvanian analysis of federal spending data); the same analysis series found Penn's federal funding fell roughly 7 percent to about $948 million in 2025.

Foundation and philanthropic funding operates at a scale well above the practice's typical clients: the $225 million Ruth and Raymond Perelman gift (2011), which named the Perelman School of Medicine, remains the largest single gift in university history, and P. Roy and Diana Vagelos gave a record $83.9 million to the School of Arts and Sciences in January 2024 [verify: Candid Foundation Directory export for the multi-year foundation grant total].

===== SECTION: Mutual Peers =====

===== SECTION: Selectivity =====
Penn is one of the most selective institutions in the country, admitting 5.4% of applicants (College Scorecard, most recent reported cycle). Selectivity is a fit signal here, not a growth-capacity gap: applicant demand, yield, and net tuition are not pain points a pitch should lead with.

===== SECTION: Designation =====
- Penn holds no MSI designations (no HSI, HBCU, TCU, AANAPISI, or PBI; College Scorecard designations are empty and intake confirms none).
- Penn is not a Title III/V-eligible profile given its resources and 16.5% Pell share [verify: ED Title III/V eligibility matrix for formal confirmation]. Eligibility-gated programs (Title III SIP, Title V) are off the table; opportunity ideas must run through open-competition programs.
