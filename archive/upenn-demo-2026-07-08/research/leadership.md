J. Larry Jameson, MD, PhD, President, University of Pennsylvania (and Trustees University Professor)

- Prior Experience:
  - University of Pennsylvania
    - Interim President (December 2023 - March 2025)
    - Executive Vice President, University of Pennsylvania for the Health System, and Dean, Raymond and Ruth Perelman School of Medicine (2011-2023)
  - Northwestern University
    - Dean, Feinberg School of Medicine, and Vice President for Medical Affairs (2007-2011)
    - Irving S. Cutter Professor of Medicine and Chair, Department of Medicine (2000-2007)
    - Chief, Division of Endocrinology, Metabolism, and Molecular Medicine (1993-2000)
  - Massachusetts General Hospital and Harvard Medical School
    - Chief, Thyroid Unit (1987-1993)
    - Harvard Medical School faculty, Instructor rising to Associate Professor of Medicine (1985-1993)
    - Internal medicine residency, endocrinology training, and Howard Hughes Medical Institute research fellowship (1981-1985)
- Education:
  - PhD, Biochemistry, University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill, 1981
  - MD (with honors), University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill, 1981
  - BS, Chemistry (with honors), University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill, 1976

Jameson is a physician-scientist and endocrinologist who led Penn Medicine for 12 years as executive vice president and dean of the Perelman School of Medicine before stepping into the presidency, first as interim president in December 2023 and then, by trustee vote on March 13, 2025, as Penn's president with a term through June 2027. He brings unusual depth on federally funded biomedical research: he is the author of more than 350 scientific articles and chapters, a longtime editor of Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, and a member of the National Academy of Medicine. His tenure has coincided with sustained federal pressure on research universities, a subject he addressed in a rare May 2026 Philadelphia Inquirer interview on leading Penn under the Trump administration. Timing matters for any pitch: on June 12, 2026 Jameson announced he will conclude his presidency in June 2027, and Penn plans to launch its presidential search in fall 2026, so Penn enters a leadership transition this cycle.

Meaney is Penn's chief research officer, overseeing what the university describes as a $1.4 billion research enterprise and the offices that run it, including the Office of Research Services (sponsored programs) and Penn's innovation and technology transfer function. A traumatic brain injury researcher on the Penn faculty since 1993, he is an NSF CAREER Award recipient whose experimental work contributed data that revised federal motor vehicle safety standards, and he has collaborated extensively with the National Football League on concussion science. As Bioengineering chair from 2007 to 2019 he tripled the department's per-capita research funding, and Penn credits strategic initiatives he led as senior associate dean with generating more than $25 million in follow-on funding across engineering, medicine, and the life sciences. He took office August 1, 2025, succeeding Dawn Bonnell, who held Penn's senior research post for 12 years (2013-2025) under the title Senior Vice Provost for Research; the post is now titled Vice Provost for Research, confirmed on Penn's OVPR staff page.

===== SECTION: Grants Office =====
Penn's central sponsored programs office is the Office of Research Services (ORS), a division of the Office of the Vice Provost for Research (OVPR), which states its mission as collaborating with the Penn community and external partners to promote meaningful scholarship, uphold research integrity, and foster new discoveries. ORS manages externally sponsored research projects through the pre-award and post-award processes, providing full life-cycle administrative support: proposal submission, award negotiation and acceptance, post-award financial administration, cost analysis, research security, export compliance, and research operations and cash management. Franklin Building, 3451 Walnut Street, 5th Floor, Philadelphia, PA; (215) 898-7293; researchservices.upenn.edu.
