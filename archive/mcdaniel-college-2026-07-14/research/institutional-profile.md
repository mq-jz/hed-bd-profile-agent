===== SECTION: About =====
McDaniel College is a private nonprofit master's institution in Westminster, Maryland, founded in 1867 as Western Maryland College and renamed in 2002. Two things make it unusual for a college of its size. It has operated a degree-granting European campus, McDaniel College Budapest, since 1994, accredited under the same Middle States accreditation as the home campus and registered with the Hungarian government as a foreign institution of higher education. And its graduate school is nearly as large as its college, 1,248 graduate students against 1,620 undergraduates (Fall 2024, IPEDS), built largely on teacher preparation for Maryland schools and anchored by a Deaf Education program run with the Maryland School for the Deaf since 1967. McDaniel is teaching-focused rather than a research university, with a master's-level highest degree and no Carnegie research designation, so the federal case here is programmatic and student-serving, not lab-driven.

===== SECTION: Mutual Peers =====
Institutions self-select their peer groups when reporting to IPEDS, and mutual peers are those that chose each other; the Chronicle of Higher Education publishes a tool that visualizes this. Of the 19 institutions in McDaniel's 2025 IPEDS comparison group, only two name McDaniel back.

- Hood College (Frederick, MD): Retainer Client - owner: Jessica Schor
- Juniata College (Huntingdon, PA): Retainer Client - owner: John McAllister

Both mutual peers are current M&Q retainer clients, and McDaniel's own Salesforce record (Non-Client) is already owned by John McAllister, who also owns Juniata.

===== SECTION: Selectivity =====
Admission rate 77.9% (College Scorecard, latest). McDaniel is lightly selective and admits the large majority of applicants, so enrollment strategy rests on yield and retention rather than on selectivity.

===== SECTION: Designation =====
No MSI or special designation. McDaniel does not appear on the statutory HBCU or TCCU lists in the U.S. Department of Education's FY 2026 Eligibility Matrix, and it holds no HSI, AANAPISI, or PBI designation. Black students are about 23.7% of enrollment, but HBCU status is statutory and historical, not enrollment-driven, and McDaniel does not qualify.

- Title III Part A (Strengthening Institutions Program): not eligible. McDaniel is absent from the 1,561 institutions on the FY 2026 Eligibility Matrix SIP list (June 8, 2026 edition). Mutual peer and current M&Q client Hood College does appear on that list, so the SIP play available at Hood is not available here.
- Title V (Developing Hispanic-Serving Institutions): not eligible. Hispanic undergraduate enrollment is 10.5%, well below the 25% statutory threshold.
